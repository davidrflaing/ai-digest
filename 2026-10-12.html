🔬 Research

  • Sakana AI and UC Berkeley, MASS: "Recursive self-improvement through collective intelligence" (arXiv 2610.12176; code at SakanaAI/mass; HN 117 / 71) — one model (Qwen3.6-27B) is solver, workflow optimizer and evaluator: an inner loop rewrites the multi-agent workflow (roles, instructions, contracts, hops) against a champion with weights fixed; an outer loop runs the champion 18 times a task, ranks outputs with Bradley–Terry, drops the workflow text and LoRA-tunes on the trajectories, then the cycle repeats. Held-out win rate against the base 53.9% → 69.9% over two rounds; score per output token 1.60× on MLR-Bench and 1.35× on DSBench but 0.96× on Terminal-Bench 2.0 and 0.94× on SWE-bench Verified; the self-evaluator's agreement with GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.8 judges rose from 73% to 93%; team runs emit about 2.4× more tokens. Flags: 12 synthetic tasks with three held out; "strong iterations can be followed by sharp regressions"; the authors flag shared blind spots reinforcing across updates. The third :learn then :consolidate-shaped result in a week (Harvey Oct 9, Memento 3 Oct 11), carrying the warning the others didn't: a homogeneous loop marks its own homework. ↗
  • Doug Turnbull, "Bag of Decisions reranker" (Oct 8; code in softwaredoug/search-experiments) — an LLM writes about ten yes/no questions per query ("does it cover desks designed for children?"), Jev answers each as a Noul over every document, and the summed P(yes) is the relevance score: NDCG mean 0.610 against 0.574 for a plain Jev rerank and 0.541 for BM25 on WANDS, and 0.360 vs 0.314 vs 0.289 on ESCI. Flags: the author calls it a toy, two e-commerce datasets, repetitive generated questions, no cost or latency reported. It reads across to the comms board directly: write the rubric for 🔴/🟡/⚪ once from triage-rules.md, then a decision model answers each line per item, which is what the aggregator-triage rules already are, as prose. ↗
