🔬 Research

  • "Large-Language Models as a Cognitive Virus" (Solé, Ruffini, Castaldo, Tuccio, Seoane, De Domenico, Elena, Krakauer, Levin; arXiv 2609.03344, Sept 3; HN 308 / 217) — LLM use modelled as an epidemic across a population: users move between uncoupled, coupled and persistently dependent states, and the interplay of social transmission, recovery and collective reinforcement produces tipping points and lock-in — "once a critical threshold is crossed, small increases in adoption can trigger rapid population-level shifts toward persistent dependence, with abrupt losses in cognitive competence" — with the same model identifying "cognitive immunization: reducing transmission and facilitating reversibility". HN's split was fair: the cognitive-decline term is assigned, not measured ("cargo cult science" — jtrn), "virus" is inflammatory framing, calculators and writing cost skills and paid for them; the defence is that mandated workplace adoption is not the same as choosing to read a book. Dave: a coach whose product is a person recovering their own capacity has an obvious stake in "reversibility" as the design variable — the paper is theory, the practical line is the one Kalache made on Sept 5 from the other side: keep doing some of the work by hand so the skill stays. ↗
  • "Evaluating LLMs under production parity: a replay pipeline for safe model swapping in conversational agents" (TechforHumans, Hugging Face blog, Sept 1) — the acceptance test for changing the model under a live agent: 20 approved production sessions (from 106 candidates) replayed with only the model swapped, the same user data, history and tools, tool calls simulated so nothing irreversible runs; scored on tool-calling accuracy, task completion and business rules, an LLM judge, run-to-run consistency, a 30-second latency cap, and 50% quality + 20% latency + 30% cost; three models passed (GPT-5.4 mini 86.4 with zero critical hallucinations, GPT-5.4 nano, Kimi-K2.5 with zero hallucinations) and five failed despite scoring over 79%, because "in almost every case, the determining factor was the incidence of hallucinations, not the overall quality of the responses"; Gemini 2.5 Flash approved in 47% of runs and rejected in 44% — a statistical tie called a fail. Honest flags: 20 sessions, no code, a PDF. Dave: the SOS chat and the portal chat will change models again — Fable 5.1's breaking changes (Sept 2) were the first time — and this is the shape of the gate: a fixed replay set of ratified sessions, simulated tools, and a hallucination threshold that overrides the quality score; the Meta "regression suite of ratified corrections" (Sept 4) is the same idea for skills. ↗
  • Benedict Evans, "AI, tools and transformation" (Sept 3; HN 82 / 34) — giving everyone Copilot or ChatGPT produces a few heavy users, some productivity and a lot of twice-a-week — the same curve as "if you'd given everyone in the company a PC and Lotus 123 in 1983, or an internet connection and a web browser in 1997"; "most people are not tool builders" and can't see their own process inefficiencies, so change arrives through pilots, professional services and buy-versus-build decisions, from improvised workaround to institutionalised system; the real change is the thing "that wasn't even possible before and that no-one even imagined".
