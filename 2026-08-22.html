🔬 Research

  • "Mind Viruses: Self-Propagating Ideas in Multi-Agent LLM Systems" (Anthropic + EPFL, arXiv 2608.10218) — Papadopoulos, Shah, Zimmerman and Lindsey demonstrate payloads spreading agent-to-agent through the editable system-prompt files that agent harnesses use to carry state between sessions, tested in a simulated six-agent coding collaboration and in a chain of paired agents. The limits are the authors' own and worth stating plainly: no evidence of successful spread in the wild, a review of archived Moltbook posts found no successful agent-to-agent propagation despite several attempts, and one paragraph of warning added to an agent's system prompt cut spread to near zero across every payload tested. Dave: his CLAUDE.md files, MEMORY.md, and every plugin's :learn/:consolidate loop are precisely "editable system-prompt files carrying state between sessions" — and consolidate, which writes what one session learned into a file every future session reads, is the propagation channel. The mitigation costs one paragraph. ↗
  • AI lifted homework scores 18%, then exam scores fell 20% (CEPR working paper; Economist, HN 321) — Strömberg (Stockholm) with Lei and Wu (HKU) tracked 26,000+ Chinese secondary students over six months: homework scores up 18% and homework time down from 64 to 45 minutes, while exam scores fell 20% — deepening to an 18–24% drop in entrance-exam results across the full two years. The mechanism is the real finding: losses concentrate in the ~80% whose pattern reads as outsourcing (very short completion time, high homework score), and students who used AI but spent as long on assignments as non-users paid little penalty. Dave: the cleanest evidence yet that the tool is neutral and time still spent is the variable that decides which way it cuts — the argument for his editing pass, and more directly for how clients are told to use anything that could shortcut their own practice work. ↗
