  • Alex Wang, "You could have invented transformers from memory" (HN 577 / 129) — one derivation: a linear layer is a key-value store with capacity about the key dimension, since interference from other memories drowns the target; least squares roughly doubles it; a ReLU power on the similarities suppresses weak matches and gives an MLP whose capacity grows polynomially; the exponential, a weighted sum of all powers, gives softmax, and the store is self-attention with one memory per context token and capacity growing exponentially with sharpness β, over a million memories at d_k = 64; scaling β with log(M) keeps recall working at long context. Dave: one line when a prospect asks what's inside: a very large associative memory, recalled with little interference, and nothing in it decides. ↗
