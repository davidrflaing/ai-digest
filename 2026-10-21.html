  • Anthropic, "Using Claude to reduce the attack surface of code" (Chris Rohlf, Frontier Red Team; Oct 20; HN 69 / 28) — instead of finding bugs, Claude maps what a default deployment can reach and gates what nothing needs: across 25 open-source projects (over 5 million lines; 10 public, 11 gated, 4 private), 17,000+ configuration options inventoried, 3,842 security-relevant, 493 issues, among them 93 unnecessary pre-authentication surfaces in default configs and 162 option combinations accepted without warning, 94 of which silently turned a control off (a hostname access rule with reverse DNS disabled failed open in four unrelated projects; one conflict had existed 21 years); the OpenSSH case: about 4,500 lines of experimental post-quantum signature code reachable by unauthenticated clients even when excluded, about 16% of pre-auth code, fixed in 10.5, two gaps Claude found in that fix closed in 10.6, roughly a fifth of the pre-auth surface gone.   • Two on routing: Meta's "The Thin Orchestrator" (Oct 19) and Parth Parikh's "To Make, or to Buy: The Economics of Decision Models" (Oct 18; HN 54 / 2) — Meta's data-onboarding agents hallucinated as one all-knowing agent; the fix is a deterministic orchestrator of about 1,300 lines that classifies intent, gathers fields, runs reliability gates and delegates to 14 subagents with isolated context, and does no domain reasoning, generates nothing and never touches the codebase; 20+ workflows are templates over the same subagent pool, 25+ knowledge files load at runtime; config prep went from 3–4 days to under an hour, about two-thirds of changes land without significant human edits, against a single agent's 70–80% with per-task prompt engineering and 10–20% without.
