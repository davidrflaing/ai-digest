🔬 Research

  • Dan Luu, "How well do agents use test/verification techniques?" (HN 117 / 44) — GPT-5.6 Sol in Codex at medium and xhigh effort, implementing Zstd in Rust and an IMAP server, 26 prompt conditions (TDD, fuzzing, differential, mutation, metamorphic and property-based testing; QuickCheck, Proptest, Hegel, rstest, insta; Verus, Alloy, Lean 4, TLA+, Kani; Z3; skills from ECC, Hegel and Trail of Bits; "make no mistakes"; nothing), ~80 runs per condition. The result: the default — no instructions at all — scored well above average, TDD did worse ("wrote more tests, worse outcomes"), formal methods proved irrelevant arithmetic properties and fell back to ordinary tests, differential testing produced two implementations with the same bug, fuzzing generated structured inputs in 10 of 160 runs, and the three published testing skills underperformed or matched nothing — "they functioned as tutorials rather than behavioral nudges"; the author's own custom skill (name the risky areas and likely mistakes first, re-derive in a fresh context, structured rather than naive randomisation) scored highest and, he says, still wasn't followed as written. His conclusion: "Agents are really bad at testing and don't seem to understand how to test reasonably by default", skills need tuning per model and effort level, and the labs haven't built the RL environments for it.
