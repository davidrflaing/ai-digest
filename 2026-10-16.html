🔬 Research

  • Nick Cammarata (Goodfire), "Neural networks are already clean" (HN 721 / 273) — an informal essay arguing that superposition, the idea that networks pack unrelated features into shared neurons, is far rarer than the field assumed, and that most apparent polysemanticity came from poor tools, weak explanations and suboptimal training: the famous "cat and car" neuron is a V-shape detector; InceptionV1's last layer went from about 5% interpretable with 2017 tools to about 50% with fixed tools, about 70% with modern regularisation, and about 99% in a same-size ConvNeXt; a preliminary audit of Qwen3-32B finds about 90% of neuron poles have one main job, about 7% two related jobs, and 2–4% unexplained, against OpenAI's 2023 figure of 1.7% interpretable; neurons squeezed from 480 to 10 got coarser, not more mixed. Flags: a working hypothesis by its own label, small samples, threshold-dependent percentages, and the "interpretability is tractable" step marked "highly speculative". Dave: one line for the sales room, where "nobody knows what's inside" comes up: that claim is weaker than it was a week ago. ↗ · HN
  • Zach Landes, "Jev meets the Trolley Problem" (Oct 14; HN 501 / 211) — 14 decision models (Jev, OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna Decisions model, eleven local ones and a baseline) on trolley variants in 24 wordings with track order varied, 5,240 setups: Jev diverts to save five like most humans, destroys its own server to save five, but kept its server over twenty people in 16 of 48 wordings (GPT-6 Luna never did; Von did in 47 of 48), pushed its server off the footbridge in 40 of 48, and changing only the order of the track descriptions flipped Jev's choice in 370 of 2,620 pairs; one model pulled the lever even with nobody on the main track. The author's conclusion is the useful one: decision models give typed outputs with probabilities and no reasons, their choices move with wording, so they "cannot be trusted out of the box and need evaluations on real data". HN: Jev isn't built for this; a commenter got 90% heads on a coin flip; no TypeSafe reply. Dave: the comms-board shadow test's variable, written out. The 🔴/🟡/⚪ prompt has to be run across paraphrases and option orders before the number means anything, and jev-latest moves to 1.14 within the week. ↗ · HN
  • Three harness papers: Stanford and Together's "Hybrid Reasoning Agents" (arXiv 2610.18861, Oct 13), Google Research's Harness² (Apache-2.0; arXiv 2610.14224), and Google's Deep Review (Oct 14) — HybridAgent reasons in latent vectors and switches to text only for tool calls and key decisions: +10.4% over frontier agents in the abstract, 35.5–54.7% fewer tokens, 1.8–2.6× faster, 2.7–3.7× cheaper across 11 benchmarks at three Qwen3-Base scales (Rohan Paul's thread: GAIA 52.4 vs 43.7 for the 14B text-only agent); code at togethercomputer/HybridAgent. Harness² keeps the weights fixed and edits the harness (system prompt, guardrails, memory, tool guidance, subagents, skills, scripts, plugins) by Propose–Probe–Compose with no ground truth, then carries "evidence-supported lessons" into an improvement harness for the next task: up to +11.5 on WorkBuddy-Bench, +4.5 on JobBench, +11.8 on LAB for GLM-5 and Gemini 3.5 Flash; Linux, Vertex AI, OpenCode or Codex as executor, with executor network access unrestricted and Codex's sandbox disabled. Deep Review is a four-stage agent (segment, budget compute by density, verify sections in parallel, synthesise and ground with search) that checks logic, maths, claims and cross-section consistency in PDFs and plain text; two free reviews a week at research.google/deepreview, more with a Gemini key; 16,000 opted-in manuscripts in conference pilots; documents stored a year, not trained on. Deep Review on a fifth-edition chapter PDF is a consistency pass before your line-by-line, on Google's servers, and canon stays with /copy-claims. ↗ · Harness² · Deep Review
