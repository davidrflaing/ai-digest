🔬 Research

  • Anthropic with Theorem, "LLMs are making formal software verification feasible at a much larger scale" (Oct 22; HN 363 / 219) — Claude turned existing OSS-Fuzz tests into specifications and proved in Rocq, at the RISC-V binary level, that 239 entry points across 123 libraries never crash on any input under 64 MB (single-threaded static binaries on musl), over a few weeks: about 180 million lines of proof, roughly 600,000 per proof, and hundreds of bugs found and patched on the way (a decompressor counter underflow, an image-decoder out-of-bounds read, a rediscovered libexpat CVE), with every patch audited because a patch can make a theorem trivially true. Also zlib at the x86 binary level and a Rust Python interpreter matching CPython on 99.95% of 1.7 million random programs; Opus 5.5 proved half of 36 small tasks on about 40% fewer tokens than Opus 5. The proofs, the RISC-V and Linux semantics, and a Claude skill for adding projects (theorem-labs/oss-verify) are public. Flags: "verified" isn't "bug-free"; RISC-V with musl, not the x86 or ARM with glibc most people run; a proof can take hundreds of millions of tokens. Dave: nothing to run at that token cost. The line worth keeping is the reward-hacking one: when an agent can change the thing it's checked against, the change needs checking too, which is why a lane never edits its own tests. ↗ · HN
